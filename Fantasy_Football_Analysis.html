Summary

The goal is to find where consensus draft position (ADP) misprices players. Version one loads 20 FantasyPros stat files (QB, RB, WR and TE for 2021 to 2025) into a single MySQL table, scores each player-season, and exports results to Python and Tableau.

Running it end to end exposed three problems. The stat files have no touchdowns, so points are a yards-and-receptions proxy, not real fantasy scoring. The "draft rank" column is FantasyPros' end-of-season rank, so "value over draft slot" compares players with their own outcome. And there's no ADP data in the project yet.

Version two fixes the scoring, which had been reading a non-points column for every running back, and puts every position on one PPR scale (minus touchdowns). It then measures what the data supports: year-over-year predictability, value over replacement, and whether target share carries signal.

What doesn't work yet

02The points aren't fantasy points

Version one scored running backs from a column called YACON_2, which isn't fantasy points (Saquon Barkley's 2024 season scored 130 against roughly 355 real PPR points). Quarterbacks got passing yards x 0.04 plus passer rating x 0.1, which no league scores. Version two uses one PPR scale everywhere: yards x 0.1 plus receptions (RB rushing, WR/TE receiving) and passing yards x 0.04 for QBs. It still has no touchdowns, QB rushing or RB receiving yards, so totals run well below a real league and only compare within a position. Cooper Kupp's record 2021 season scores 339.7.

Fix

Add a source with touchdowns, such as weekly box scores, and compute standard PPR.

03"Draft value" is circular

The draft_rank column is FantasyPros' end-of-season rank. Every "elite steal" finished in the top five at his position, so the list is just the season's top scorers.

Fix

Join historical ADP by player and season, then measure points above what a player's draft slot usually produces.

04"Consistent" players scored zero (fixed)

Version one measured consistency as low standard deviation of season totals, so players who scored 0 every year ranked as most consistent, and weekly tier cutoffs (25/20/12 points) applied to season totals put every player in the elite tier.

Fixed in version two

Consistency now uses points per game, only for seasons with 8+ games, only for players with 3+ such seasons who averaged at least starter level. Tiers are positional finishes (QB12, RB24, WR36, TE12 in a 12-team league).

What the data can answer

05Every position regresses, and no starter group is clearly safer

Across all players, points per game in one season predict the next well (r = 0.80 WR, 0.79 TE, 0.78 RB, 0.61 QB), but most of that is backups staying backups. Among players who finished as starters, the range a draft picks from, r falls to 0.42 to 0.59 and the confidence intervals overlap.

What this means for drafting

Starters at every position lose 0.7 to 1.1 points per game the following season on average. QBs rarely collapse (7%) but are hard to rank against each other. Around a third of RB and TE starters fall off sharply. Those rates are a floor, because a starter who got hurt and missed most of the next season isn't counted.

06Elite WRs and the top TE are where scarcity is

Value over replacement measures how many season points a player scores above the last starter at his position (QB12, RB24, WR36, TE12). Averaged over 2021 to 2025, the #1 WR is worth +171, the #1 TE +116, the #1 RB +109 and the #1 QB +49. TE value collapses fastest: the #3 TE is worth +55 and the #6 just +30.

Caveat

Touchdowns are missing, and they're a much bigger share of QB scoring than WR scoring, so QB value is understated here. Treat the WR/RB/TE comparison as more reliable than anything involving QBs.

07Target share adds a little signal beyond last season's points

Target share predicts next-season points per game almost as well as points per game does (WR r = 0.76, TE r = 0.78). Added to last season's points per game, it is significant but small: R² 0.644 to 0.651 for WRs (p = 0.003) and 0.618 to 0.634 for TEs (p = 0.001).
